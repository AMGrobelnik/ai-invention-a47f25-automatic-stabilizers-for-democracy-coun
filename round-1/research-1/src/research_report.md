## Research Findings

Four sub-questions were investigated with the following findings:

**SQ1 — Prior work connecting redistribution cyclicality to democratic outcomes: ZERO papers found [1][2][3].**

All adjacent literature branches away from the proposed hypothesis. Rau & Stokes (2025) [1] is the closest paper: it uses the post-tax-post-transfer net Gini (a redistribution LEVEL, not a cyclical measure) in a static logit hazard of democratic erosion onset, using Laebens erosion coding from V-Dem (1995-2020, 23 spells in 22 countries). No shock design, no cyclical component, and no impulse-response. McKay & Reis (2016) [2] establishes that automatic stabilizers reduce income-inequality volatility in a quantitative HANK model, but the outcome is macroeconomic welfare — no democratic or political variable appears. Janus, Riera-Crichton & Tarufelli (2022) [3] uses commodity ToT shocks to predict political transitions FROM AUTOCRACY TO DEMOCRACY — the opposite direction from democratic erosion — with no redistribution mechanism studied. IMF WP 2025 Arezki, Imam & Kpodar [4] uses Gruss-Kebhaj CTOT shocks to predict adoption of fiscal rules and financial openness, not democratic quality or redistribution cyclicality.

**SQ2 — LP-based democratic resilience metrics: NO precedent found [5][6].**

All existing democratic resilience empirics use binary event models. Boese et al. (2021) [5] uses a two-stage binary process (ERT dataset): Stage 1 is avoiding erosion onset; Stage 2 is averting breakdown. Economic development matters for Stage 1 only. Rau & Stokes (2025) [1] uses a logit hazard (discrete, static). Funke, Schularick & Trebesch (2016) [6] is the nearest methodological comp — using local projections to trace political outcomes (far-right vote share) after banking crisis onset — but their LP measures political DAMAGE following a shock, not democratic resilience recovery half-life as the dependent variable. Using LP to define resilience as the number of periods to recover 50% of an initial shock-induced V-Dem decline has no direct precedent.

**SQ3 — SWIID cyclical proxy vs. OECD direct estimates [7][8][9].**

Direct automatic-stabilizer semi-elasticity estimates exist only for: 28 EU Member States (Mourre et al. 2019) [7] and 35 OECD countries (Maravalle & Rawdanowicz 2020) [8]. Latin America, non-EU Eastern Europe, Sub-Saharan Africa, and Central Asia — comprising the bulk of the democratizer sample — have NO direct automatic-stabilizer estimates. SWIID (Solt 2020, v9.92, April 2026) [9] provides absolute redistribution (market Gini minus disposable Gini) for 199 countries. The cyclical component (HP-filter extraction) can serve as a within-country redistribution-cyclicality proxy. Critical caveat: SWIID redistribution is reliable only in country-years where both market and disposable income distributions are directly observed in source data. Recommended strategy: full-sample regressions with SWIID cyclical proxy; robustness check replacing SWIID with Mourre et al. semi-elasticities on the EU-28 subsample.

**SQ4 — Best-practice commodity ToT shock instruments [4][10][11].**

Gruss & Kebhaj (2019) IMF WP 2019/021 [4] is the primary recommended instrument: 182 economies, 1962-2018, 45 commodities, fixed-weight version preferred for exogeneity (severs price-quantity feedback). Already used in political-outcome research (Janus et al. 2022 [3]; IMF WP 2025 [4]). Bazzi & Blattman (2014) AEJ:Macro [10] provides an alternative construction using lagged trade-share weights (original context: conflict identification, main finding: no effect on conflict onset). Laeven & Valencia (2020) [11] Systemic Banking Crises Database II (151 episodes, 1970-2017, updated 2026 to 1970-2025) offers a binary banking crisis onset instrument, already used by Funke et al. (2016) [6] for political economy outcomes.

**Novelty conclusion**: The intersection of {redistribution cyclicality} × {LP recovery half-life as resilience metric} × {commodity ToT + banking crisis shock identification} is unoccupied in the published literature as of June 2026. The gap is confirmed and three-dimensional.

[4] [Gruss & Kebhaj (2019) — Commodity Terms of Trade: A New Database (IMF WP 2019/021)](https://www.imf.org/en/Publications/WP/Issues/2019/03/26/Commodity-Terms-of-Trade-46197) — Commodity ToT indices for 182 economies, 1962-2018, 45 commodities. [7] [Mourre et al.
