## Full Summary

Four sub-questions were investigated. (1) Zero prior papers connect redistribution cyclicality or automatic-stabilizer strength to political/democratic outcomes. Adjacent papers study redistribution LEVEL in static logit models (Rau & Stokes 2025 PNAS) or commodity shocks affecting regime transitions from autocracy (Janus et al. 2022 Economics & Politics), not erosion from democracy via a redistribution channel. (2) No LP-based or recovery-rate democratic resilience metric exists in published work. All extant resilience empirics use binary hazard/logit models (Boese et al. 2021 ERT; Rau & Stokes 2025); Funke et al. (2016 EER) is the nearest method comp — using local projections to trace political damage after financial crises — but does not measure democracy-quality recovery half-life as the dependent variable. (3) Direct automatic-stabilizer semi-elasticity estimates are limited to 28 EU Member States (Mourre et al. 2019 EU DP 098) or 35 OECD countries (Maravalle & Rawdanowicz 2020 OECD WP 1636); Latin America, non-EU Eastern Europe, and Sub-Saharan Africa have no direct estimates. SWIID cyclical redistribution proxy (Solt 2020, v9.92) is the only tractable measure for the full democratizer sample, validated against Mourre et al. on the EU subsample. (4) Gruss-Kebhaj (2019) IMF WP 2019/021 fixed-weight CTOT (182 economies, 1962-2018, 45 commodities) is the recommended primary instrument; Bazzi-Blattman (2014 AEJ:Macro) provides a robustness alternative; Laeven-Valencia (2020) banking crisis onset is an additional binary shock instrument used by Funke et al. (2016) for political outcomes. The novelty gap is three-dimensional: cyclical redistribution (not level) x LP recovery half-life (not binary hazard) x commodity ToT plus banking crisis instruments (not previously applied to this question).
